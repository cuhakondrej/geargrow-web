Welcome to GearGrow

What this site is for: it helps parents work out what size of clothes, shoes and sports gear their child will need next season, so they can buy ahead and potentially save.

Sizes are recommendations only. They are based on the growth curves of the World Health Organization (WHO) and on scientific studies of foot growth. They cannot predict with 100% certainty how your child will grow. Treat them as a guide, not a guarantee, and compare the size with the brand's size chart before you buy.

We don't store your child's data. Everything you enter is calculated on your device only and is not sent anywhere. We count visits anonymously, without cookies.

Guide

How to choose the size of a kids' helmet

Helmets are sold by head circumference in centimetres, for example 48–52 cm or 52–56 cm. Labels such as XS, S or M differ between brands, so always go by the centimetres. After age five the head grows slowly, so a helmet that adjusts well can last two to three seasons.

How to measure the head

Wrap a tape measure around the widest part of the head: about 1 cm above the eyebrows at the front, above the ears at the sides and over the fullest part at the back. Don't pull it tight. Measure twice and go by the larger number.

Which range to choose

The head circumference should be within the helmet's range, ideally in the middle or lower half. That leaves room to grow. If the head is right on the border between two sizes, try both. The dial at the back fine-tunes the fit.

How a helmet should fit

  • It sits level, with the front edge about two fingers above the eyebrows.
  • Once buckled and adjusted, it doesn't move when the child shakes their head.
  • The straps form a V under the ears and one finger fits under the chin strap.

Bike and ski helmets

A bike helmet should meet the EN 1078 standard, a ski helmet EN 1077. Try a ski helmet on with the goggles: there should be no gap between helmet and goggles. Replace a helmet after a crash with an impact, even if you can't see any damage.

Frequently asked questions

What size helmet for a 3-year-old?

The average head circumference of a 3-year-old is about 49 cm. A helmet whose range includes 49 cm fits, for example 46–51 cm or 48–52 cm. Heads differ by several centimetres, though, so measure before you buy.

Can I buy a helmet for my child to grow into?

Only within the helmet's range: choose one where today's head circumference is in the middle or lower half of the range. A helmet that is too big shifts in a fall and doesn't protect the head.

What do sizes XS, S and M mean for kids' helmets?

Every brand defines them differently. What counts is the head circumference range in centimetres that the maker gives for each size.

When should a kids' helmet be replaced?

After any significant impact, when the head outgrows the helmet's range, and, as makers usually recommend, after three to five years of use.

Can a bike helmet be used for skiing?

It isn't recommended. A ski helmet to EN 1077 covers more of the back of the head and the ears, is built for the cold and fits with ski goggles.

How it works

Three ways we work out the size

We size what you choose by height, by foot length or by age. The forecast is based on the growth curves of the World Health Organization (WHO) and on scientific studies of children's growth. The result is always a range, not a single number, because children don't grow perfectly evenly. When a child is between two sizes, we show both.

Height

Clothes, bikes, skis

We work out how much the child will grow by the chosen date and size clothes, bikes, skis, snowboards or poles from that. For babies we start from birth. If you enter the weight, chest or waist girth, we also take the build into account for clothes. From 8 years you can also enter the parents' heights and an earlier height, which make the growth forecast in puberty more precise.

Foot

All footwear and skates

We work out how much the foot will grow by the date and add room for the type of footwear, that is, how much longer the shoe should be than the foot:

Hockey skates+4 mm
Ice skates, gym shoes+5–7 mm
Football boots, indoor shoes+8 mm
Trainers, hiking boots+12 mm
Winter boots, wellies+15 mm

The recommended size already includes the room; don't add anything.

Age

Balls, gloves, pads

Things sold by age group are sized by the child's age at the date. Helmets and hats by head circumference.
